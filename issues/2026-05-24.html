This Week's Stories

Anthropic Launches Claude for Small Business — and the SaaS Stack Starts to Look Exposed

Anthropic / TechCrunch / Yahoo Finance Industry

On May 13, Anthropic launched Claude for Small Business — a plugin inside Claude Cowork aimed not at enterprises but at "the local hardware store or coffee shop." It ships with 15 pre-built workflows that activate on a toggle install — no prompts to write, no configuration. Connectors at launch: QuickBooks, PayPal, HubSpot, Canva, Docusign, Google Workspace, and Microsoft 365. The workflows are the part to watch: payroll planning, 30-day cash forecasting, month-end close, overdue invoice chasing, contract review, lead triage, margin analysis, employee onboarding — every recurring task a small-business owner currently does manually at 10pm or pays a SaaS tool to make easier.

The strategic threat is not that Claude replaces QuickBooks. It's that Claude becomes the layer the owner actually interacts with, and QuickBooks becomes the database underneath. That changes who captures the customer, who renews the contract, and who sets the price. Wall Street has already read the signal: Salesforce, ServiceNow, Intuit, DocuSign, and Box have all underperformed year-to-date. Dario Amodei warned at Anthropic's recent financial services event that "some SaaS companies will go bankrupt" if they don't keep pace. The orchestration layer captures more value than the system of record below it — and the frontier labs are racing for that layer right now.

The Token Meter Is Creeping Onto Your AI Bill — and PowerPoint Is Where You'll Notice First

Axios / Yahoo Finance / OpenAI / Anthropic / Writer Industry

The all-you-can-eat AI subscription is breaking, and the math is no longer subtle. Sam Altman publicly admitted OpenAI is losing money on its $200/month ChatGPT Pro plan because people use it more than expected — a price Altman personally set, believing it would be profitable. OpenAI's projected 2024 losses were ~$5B against $3.7B in revenue, driven primarily by the compute cost of running ChatGPT. The $200 plan isn't a margin product; it's a loss-leader for serious users.

Anthropic faces the same physics and has been quieter about it, but the response is visible in the product. Since 2026, Pro plan users hit walls faster than a year ago, API rate limits feel more restrictive, and heavy Claude Code sessions burn through allowances in hours rather than days — the result of Anthropic underinvesting in compute relative to demand, with new infrastructure 12–24 months out. In May, Anthropic moved third-party agent tool usage behind a separate credit meter on paid Claude plans. Axios's framing: "all-you-can-eat" AI subscriptions may not survive the agent era, where software can burn through computing resources far faster than humans ever could. Anthropic's Enterprise tier is the leading indicator — every token used in chat, Claude Code, or Cowork is metered at standard API rates on top of seat cost, with no included usage. The frontier labs are quietly migrating their best customers off flat-rate billing.

The place this lands hardest in practice is PowerPoint. OpenAI launched a ChatGPT add-in for PowerPoint on May 21, globally across all tiers. Anthropic has offered the same through Claude since September. Gemini integrates with Slides. Three frontier labs, same conclusion: chat was a transitional UI, and the real surface area is Office. Building a deck is also extraordinarily token-intensive — the model has to research, reason about layout, generate copy, generate visuals, and iterate against templates. Practitioner verdict in May 2026: Writer and Claude are doing this better than the rest today (Writer in particular for enterprise governance and brand-fidelity; Claude for the writing layer). One reviewer testing the same prompt across Claude desktop, Cowork, and Claude Code on Opus 4.7 got slides generated on top of a default theme every time — master slides, theme, and color palette untouched. The same prompt to GPT-5.5 via Codex produced what was asked for in one shot: master slides, theme colors, layouts, the whole thing. The honest read: GPT-5.5 plus Codex is closing the design gap fast; the next two product cycles will likely close it for ChatGPT Image 2.0 + Codex as well.

For a CAIO, the planning implication is concrete. Token economics now belongs alongside vendor risk and supply-chain resilience in any AI deployment plan. Three questions every AI tool launch should answer: which model handles which task (Haiku vs. Opus is a 5–25× cost swing per token), what gets cached versus regenerated, and how usage gets attributed to teams and projects. Agentic workloads make this urgent — a single autonomous agent running all day can cost between $1,000 and $5,000 under metered billing, which is a different conversation than a $20 seat. The companies that treat AI as a flat subscription are about to discover their best users are also their most expensive.

The CMO's AI Decision: From Content Speed to Operating-Model Reinvention

A companion executive briefing for CMOs, Chief Growth Officers, Chief Digital Officers, Chief Data Officers, General Counsel, and CEOs.

Heads-up: A short TL;DR, then an interactive briefing dashboard embedded below. The dashboard is the main deliverable this week — navigate the 9 sections by the sidebar inside it.
Disclaimer: All opinions in this briefing are my own. They do not represent the company's position. This is meant as an academic discussion of marketing AI transformation.

TL;DR

Enterprise marketing has reached AI ubiquity — but not AI value. McKinsey's State of AI 2025 (1,993 respondents, ~105 countries) finds 88% of organizations now use AI in at least one function, with marketing and sales among the top adopters. Yet only approximately 6% qualify as "AI high performers" — defined as reporting both more than 5% of EBIT attributable to AI and significant enterprise value from AI. The differentiator between adopters and winners is people redesigning work alongside AI — not tool count, and not headcount cuts: McKinsey finds 55% of high performers report fundamental workflow redesign vs. only 20% of all other respondents.

The leading edge has shifted from "AI for content" to four harder bets: (1) an industrial AI-enabled content supply chain (Unilever, Coca-Cola, Klarna); (2) agentic campaign operations (BCG: marketing leaders expect AI agents to handle more than one-fifth of marketing's workload within two to three years); (3) federated brand-governed AI (Unilever's Brand DNAi, Coca-Cola's Digital Council); and (4) the CMO as enterprise growth-transformation lead — IBM IBV's 2025 study of 1,800 executives finds 64% of marketing leaders now own profitability and 58% own revenue growth.

Speed has outpaced governance, and that gap is now the dominant risk. The FTC's 2024 Fake Reviews Rule (effective October 21, 2024; civil penalties up to $53,088 per violation in 2025), the EU AI Act's prohibited-practices regime (fines up to 7% of global annual turnover), and SEC AI-washing enforcement have together redrawn the marketing AI red lines. Gartner finds CMOs allocate an average of 15.3% of marketing budgets to AI, yet only 30% report mature AI readiness — and 71.6% of marketers who have AI policies have not established ROI targets for those investments.

The cautionary lesson is Klarna. After publicly cutting service capacity in 2024 with AI substitution, the company began re-hiring human agents in 2025 after CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski acknowledged that AI-only service had produced lower quality. The consensus across the report's evidence base is now clear: AI works as augmentation. Pure substitution fails.

Stop benchmarking AI adoption by tool count. Start benchmarking by the question every consulting firm in the bibliography is converging on: how much of your marketing workload has been redesigned around AI, and what share of growth is now attributable to it? By 2027, the difference between marketing functions that thrived through the AI transition and those that did not will not be access to models — every CMO will have access to the same frontier models, platforms, and agencies. The difference will be in workflow redesign depth, upstream governance, and whether the CMO claimed the enterprise growth leadership mandate that the AI moment is offering.
